The problem

The checkout worked, but it didn't always tell the truth.

What I did

I redesigned how it looks and how it flows.

And I led its AI-assisted build. Payments, shipping and guest checkout had already been built by a teammate.

The result

In production in 9 days.

Five parts, each delivered separately.

Tools: Claude, interactive HTML prototypes, Linear and GitHub.

Decision 1 of 4

Every completed step stays in view.

Before, what you'd entered disappeared and, to change it, you had to go back step by step. Now it stays at the top, with “Edit”.

Decision 2 of 4

Shipping shows its real cost.

Before, it could say “ARS 0.00” even when shipping wasn't in the total. Now, “To be determined” until it's chosen, and “Free” only when it really costs nothing.

Decision 3 of 4

The final screen says what really happened.

Before, it said “Purchase Successful!” with any method, even with an unconfirmed transfer. Now, one title per situation, and “Purchase successful” only when the payment is approved.

Decision 4 of 4

On mobile, the button always within reach.

Before, the button sat at the end, below the products. Now it's in a sticky, full-width bar, with the collapsed summary that opens when you tap it.

As a team

Five parts, following the team's rules.

Each one was reviewed separately and merged following the team's conventions for branches, titles and descriptions. When a teammate's code changed midway, I kept their changes and listed them in the PR.

The production test

I went through it as a buyer and fixed five things.

It was the night it launched. All five depended on real data, which only existed in production.

  1. An autofilled phone number with a leading 0 lost its last digit.
  2. Signing in with a real Google account didn't return to the checkout.
  3. The phone number saved in the account wasn't used.
  4. The pickup address entered by the seller showed up twice.
  5. When signed out, the cart didn't show.

One small fix per problem, each with a test that failed before and passed after.
